# Designing the future of mobility — for everyone

<div class="row" markdown="0">
  <p class="lead-text">
  Advanced mobility — automated vehicles, cooperative traffic, and urban air mobility — promises
  safer and more inclusive transport, but only if people understand these systems, trust them
  appropriately, and can actually use them. Our research spans three connected pillars:
  <strong>accessibility</strong>, <strong>mobility</strong>, and <strong>computational modelling and
  simulation</strong>. We combine empirical user studies (in VR, simulators, and the field) with
  data-driven and optimisation-based methods so that interfaces can be evaluated and improved at
  scale — frequently before the first participant arrives.
  </p>
</div>

  <h4 style="overflow: hidden"> Accessibility &amp; Inclusive Design </h4>
  <p style="overflow: hidden"> Automated vehicles communicate with the world around them through external
  Human-Machine Interfaces (eHMIs) — but most assume a sighted, hearing pedestrian. We design and
  evaluate inclusive alternatives, from
  <a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376472" target="_blank" rel="noopener">external communication for pedestrians with vision impairments</a>
  to tactile and auditory in-vehicle interfaces that convey traffic information to blind and
  low-vision passengers. The goal is mobility technology that is usable, safe, and dignified for
  everyone — not just the average user. </p>
</div>

<div class="rowl1" markdown="0">
  <img src="{{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/images/respic/mobility.png" alt="Path visualisations in automated urban air mobility" class="img-responsive" width="45%" style="float: right; border-radius:7px" />
  <h4 style="overflow: hidden"> Mobility &amp; Automated Vehicle Interaction </h4>
  <p style="overflow: hidden"> We study how people interact with — and around — automated vehicles:
  pedestrians, cyclists, manual drivers, and passengers. Our work covers external communication and
  its <a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3596248" target="_blank" rel="noopener">scalability</a> when many
  vehicles share a scene, cooperative driving and platooning, trust calibration through
  visualisation of vehicle perception and intent, and the emerging domain of
  <a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3596249" target="_blank" rel="noopener">urban air mobility</a>. We use
  high-fidelity Virtual Reality and driving-simulator studies to evaluate interactions safely before
  they reach the road. </p>
</div>

<div class="rowl1" markdown="0">
  <img src="{{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/images/respic/computational.png" alt="OptiCarVis: Bayesian-optimised automated-vehicle visualisations" class="img-responsive" width="45%" style="float: left; border-radius:7px" />
  <h4 style="overflow: hidden; padding: 0px 16px"> Computational Modelling &amp; Simulation </h4>
  <p style="padding: 0px 16px"> Traditional user-centred design is slow and expensive. We make it more
  efficient with computational methods: <strong>multi-objective Bayesian optimisation</strong> to
  automatically design and tune vehicle and eHMI visualisations
  (<a href="https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3713514" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OptiCarVis</a>),
  and large-scale traffic simulation to study how interaction designs play out across whole streets
  and cities (<a href="https://github.com/M-Colley/pedsumo" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PedSUMO</a>).
  These models let us predict user experience and behaviour, and explore design spaces that would be
  impractical to test by hand. </p>
</div>
